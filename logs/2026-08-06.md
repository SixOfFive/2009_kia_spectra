# 2026-08-06 — low-voltage auto-start (opt-in), start log, CPU load (fw 2.4/2.5)

The feature that was deliberately held back since June is now built —
**shipping disabled**, with a dashboard toggle to arm it when the unit
goes in the car. Plus per-core CPU load, and a real BOM bug fixed.

## Auto-start — the whole point of the project

Once armed, the firmware fires the Compustar START code by itself after
battery voltage stays below a threshold for a sustained period, so the
alternator can put charge back before the battery is too flat to crank.

Defaults: **below 12.4 V, held 60 s**, 2 h cooldown. All of it is
configurable from the dashboard and persists in NVS.

### Why 12.4 V and not the 12.2 V in the old MicroPython tree

Two reasons, both specific to a car parked outside in Alberta:

- **Cold cranking.** Near −20 °C the engine wants roughly double the
  torque while the battery delivers about half its rated power, so the
  "still cranks" line moves up a couple of tenths from the mild-weather
  figure.
- **Electrolyte freezing.** 12.2 V resting is about SG 1.19, which
  slushes up around −26 °C — right at the local design low. 12.4 V is
  ~SG 1.23, good to about −37 °C. Firing at 12.2 can mean firing at a
  battery already too cold-soaked to turn the engine.

In a mild climate 12.2 V is perfectly reasonable; it's one field on the
page. The UI help text explains both.

### The 60 s hold

Cranking drags terminal voltage to ~9–10 V for a second or two. A full
minute of *continuous* sub-threshold reading is far longer than any
crank or momentary load (blower, headlights), so a real start can never
be mistaken for a flat battery. Any reading above the threshold resets
the timer, so "held for 60 s" really means continuously.

### Guards — all must pass before a packet goes out

Enabled · RF armed · ≥2 min since boot · reading inside **8–16 V** ·
strictly below threshold for the hold time · ≥15 min below 13.2 V
(alternator off ⇒ parked, so it can't fire while driving) · cooldown
elapsed · battery recovered ≥12.55 V for 10 min since the last start ·
under 3 starts in 24 h · not locked out.

Three of those came out of the design review and are worth calling out:

- **8–16 V sanity band.** Outside it the reading is treated as *no
  information*, never as "low". This is what stops the bench rig (which
  reads 3.27 V off the board's own rail) from counting down and firing.
- **Re-arm hysteresis.** Without it a battery that recovers to just
  above the trigger re-fires every cooldown, forever, until the tank is
  empty.
- **Park-confirm.** Voltage must sit below 13.2 V for 15 min before it
  can arm, which is what keeps it from firing while you're driving.

### Did the engine actually catch?

After any start the firmware watches 3 min for the alternator to come
up — the only real proof. Recorded per event as *ran* / *no charge*.
Two **automatic** starts in a row with no charge latch a `lockout`: the
car isn't going to start, so stop cranking it. A **manual** press never
counts toward that lockout (verified on the bench — see below).

## Start history

Every start, automatic or manual, is logged to LittleFS `/starts.bin`
(64-entry ring, written through immediately so a cranking brownout
can't lose it) and shown at the bottom of the dashboard: date/time,
voltage at trigger, auto vs manual, whether the TX went out, and
whether the engine ran. `GET /starts` for JSON, `POST /starts` clears.

## CPU load

Real per-core utilisation, not a proxy: the Arduino core ships with
`CONFIG_FREERTOS_GENERATE_RUN_TIME_STATS` + `USE_TRACE_FACILITY`
enabled and counters clocked off `esp_timer`, so load is just the share
of wall time each core's IDLE task didn't get. Two new dashboard tiles
plus `cpu0` / `cpu1` in `/json`.

Deliberately **not** the usual "spin a low-priority counter task"
trick — that keeps a runnable task on the CPU forever, defeating
tick-idling and quietly raising the parked-car draw.

Measured: ~10–13 % idle at 240 MHz; core 1 hit **67 %** while being
hammered with `/history` requests (core 1 is where `loop()` runs).

## BOM fix — the divider values were wrong

`docs/BOM.md` row 7 listed **30 kΩ + 10 kΩ**. That's a ÷4 divider: 14.6 V
of alternator charging would land at **3.65 V** on the ADC pin — past
the ~3.1 V top of the ESP32-S3's 12 dB range and uncomfortably near the
3.3 V rail. The built hardware is **1 MΩ + 220 kΩ** (÷5.545), which puts
14.6 V at 2.63 V and reads usefully to ~17 V. Corrected, with a note
explaining the headroom, the 1 MΩ's role as GPIO overvoltage protection,
and why the 100 nF must sit physically at the pin.

## Verified on the bench

Board battery is disconnected in the car, so firing was safe to test.

- Compiles clean: **1126979 bytes (35 %)**, no warnings. OTA 2.3 → 2.4 → **2.5**.
- Defaults on first boot: disabled, 12.40 V, 60 s, 7200 s, no lockout.
- **Armed on the bench it refuses to fire** — 120 s `warmup`, then
  settles at `no-battery` (3.27 V is outside the 8–16 V band) and
  `park_s` never accumulates. This is the guard that matters most.
- Config validation: `volts=9.0`, `volts=14.0`, `hold=5`, `cool=60` all
  rejected 400. Valid changes accepted and **persisted through a reflash**.
- Manual START fired and logged with a real NTP timestamp, `src=manual`,
  `ver=0` pending → flipped to **`ver=2` (no charge)** after 180 s, with
  `as_fails` staying **0** — confirming a manual test press can't latch
  the automatic system off.
- CPU load responsive under real load (see above).
- Left in a clean state: auto-start **disabled**, defaults restored, test
  log cleared, CPU back to 240 MHz, RF still armed for manual use.

**Not yet exercised:** the actual auto-fire path end to end. It can't be
on the bench — the sanity band correctly refuses a 3.27 V reading, and
the config range won't go low enough to meet it. That gets its real test
once the divider is on the car battery.

## fw 2.6 — 24 h cap made optional, CPU graphs

### The cap was wrong, and the reason matters

I'd put a hard cap of 3 auto-starts per 24 h in as runaway protection.
Removed — it's now **optional and off by default** (`0` or `−1` =
unlimited, configurable from the dashboard).

The context that settles it: this car has a **parasitic drain that has
already destroyed two batteries**, and winters here routinely kill
batteries by cold. The battery currently in it is new, and is being
*physically disconnected* between uses as a manual workaround. Finding
the leak is expensive — the labour to trace circuits is significant and
few shops go that deep — so this device *is* the fix, not a stopgap.

Given that, a count cap means deliberately choosing to let the battery
sit flat once it's hit. That's the precise failure the project exists to
prevent, and deep-discharge sulfation is exactly what killed the last
two. Weighed against it, the cost of extra starts is small: a starter is
good for on the order of tens of thousands of cycles, and a handful of
extra ones a day is a rounding error against a $200 battery every year.

The runaway guard that actually works is the **lockout**: two starts in
a row drawing no charge means the engine isn't catching, so more
cranking achieves nothing and it latches off. That distinguishes "this
battery legitimately needs frequent help" from "something is broken" —
a fixed count can't.

### Re-arm hysteresis had the same flaw

The re-arm bar was a fixed 12.55 V. On a battery that could only recover
to, say, 12.5 V, `needRearm` would never clear and auto-start would
**silently stop working** — the same "refuses to run when you need it"
failure. Now:

- **Relative** to the trigger (`threshold + 0.15 V`), so it tracks if the
  threshold is changed.
- **Times out** after 2 cooldowns and re-arms anyway. Hysteresis may
  delay a start; it must never be able to block one permanently.

### CPU graphs

Two more 24 h charts (core 0, core 1) with the same hover tooltips — the
hover is driven by the `IDS`/`CHARTS` arrays, so new canvases inherit it.
`Sample` gained two `uint8_t` CPU fields (free — they fit in existing
struct padding), averaged across the whole 60 s interval rather than
whichever 2 s window landed on the sample. History magic `VOL3`→`VOL4`,
so the 24 h buffer is discarded once on this flash.

### Verified

- Compiles clean, **1129011 bytes (35 %)**. OTA 2.5 → **2.6**.
- `max24`: `-1`→unlimited, `0`→unlimited, `5`→cap of 5, `300`→400. Default 0.
- `/history` header now `…,rssi,cpu0,cpu1`.

## fw 2.7 — native SNMP agent (Cacti can poll the board directly)

Asked whether SNMP was already set up for Cacti. It was — but **only on
the Pi** (`pi/app/snmp_responder.py`, subtree `.99999.7`), and that's
the v2 telematics half that's parked. So in the shipped v1 build there
was nothing for Cacti to poll. Built one into the firmware.

- `snmp_agent.h/.cpp` — read-only SNMPv1/v2c on **UDP 161**, hand-rolled
  BER (~300 lines, no library). **GET / GETNEXT / GETBULK. No SET path
  exists at all.** Wrong community is dropped silently, like snmpd.
- Subtree **`1.3.6.1.4.1.99999.8`** — deliberately `.8` so it can't
  collide with the Pi responder's `.7` if both ever run.
- **39 OIDs**, covering everything on the dashboard: battery mV, ADC mV,
  chip temp (deci-°C), uptime, RSSI, heap/PSRAM/disk, HTTP byte
  counters, CPU clock + both core loads, WiFi power-save, NTP state, RF
  status, and the full auto-start block (enabled, threshold, hold,
  cooldown, state, countdown, lockout, fail count, fires/24 h, park
  timer, start count, last-start epoch).
- Floats scaled to integers (millivolts, deci-degrees) — SNMP has no
  float type and Cacti is happier with an integer plus a CDEF.

### The bug worth recording

First flash **rebooted the board on the third SNMP request**. Cause: the
BER buffers were stack locals totalling ~7 KB, and the Arduino loop task
gets an 8 KB stack — straight overflow. Made them `static` (poll() is
only ever called from loop(), single-threaded, so that's safe).

Collateral: the crash-reboot loop discarded an NVS page, silently
rolling `as_volts` and `cpu_mhz` back to older values. That looked like
a persistence bug, so it got tested explicitly afterwards — set 12.4 V /
240 MHz, forced a reboot, both came back correctly. Persistence is fine;
the rollback was crash fallout, not a code fault.

### Verified with an independent client

Wrote a stdlib Python SNMP client that re-implements BER from the spec
rather than sharing code with the firmware, so agreement is real
evidence:

- Full walk of the subtree returns **39 OIDs**, correct types
  (Gauge32 / Counter32 / TimeTicks / INTEGER / STRING) and plausible
  values.
- `GET` of a single OID works; `GET` of a nonexistent leaf returns the
  v2c **`noSuchObject`** exception rather than an error-status.
- `GETBULK` with max-repetitions=5 returns exactly 5 varbinds.
- Wrong community → silently dropped.
- Every value cross-checked against `/json` — they agree field for field.

Worth noting for the parasitic-drain problem: `.5.0` (battery mV)
graphed over days *is* the diagnostic. The slope is the drain rate, and
it shows whether pulling a given fuse changed anything — which turns an
expensive shop-labour problem into a patient DIY one.

## fw 2.9 — battery drain rate, the actual leak-hunting tool

Least-squares fit of voltage against time over the most recent **parked**
window. The backwards walk stops at any sample where the alternator was
running (>= 13.2 V), at an implausible reading, or at a >10 min hole in
the timestamps (board was off) — so a drive cycle or a power gap can't
contaminate the slope. Two passes rather than buffering, keeping ~11 KB
of arrays off the heap.

Reports signed **mV/h**, **r2**, sample count, window span, and projected
**days to 11.8 V**. New dashboard card under the voltage graph, new
`drain_*` JSON fields, new SNMP OIDs `.26`–`.29` (microvolts/hour as a
plain signed integer so Cacti needs no scaling).

**r2 is surfaced deliberately.** Over a short window the slope is mostly
ADC noise, so the card says "too noisy to trust yet" below 0.6 rather
than showing a confident wrong number. Validated in Python against
synthetic series: with realistic +/-4 mV noise a -5 mV/h drain needs
~4 h to reach r2 0.86 and ~12 h for 0.98. Clean series recover exactly
(-5 and -20 mV/h, r2=1.000) and the days-to-flat projection matches hand
calculation; a charging series correctly reports no projection.

Live on hardware after 30 samples: `+3.37 mV/h, r2=0.059, 2256 s window`
— i.e. a flat bench rail correctly read as "no trend, don't trust this",
with no projection since it isn't discharging. SNMP `.26.0` = 3366 uV/h
matches `/json` exactly.

**Practical use for the parasite:** pull one fuse, let it sit at least
4 h, compare mV/h against the previous run. That turns "does this trace
look flatter?" into a number, which is the difference between a
diagnosis and a hunch.

## RF remote-start bring-up — SDR-guided, fw 3.1 -> 4.1

Long thread this session: the transmitter would not crank the car despite
the CC1101 self-test passing and every register reading back correct. Root-
caused with the RTL-SDR to a chain of five separate defects, each fixed and
verified on-air:

1. **fw 3.1** — TX strobed the carrier then clocked data immediately; the
   IDLE->TX auto-calibration (~721 us) ate the leading sync. Fixed by polling
   MARCSTATE == 0x13 before the first data edge (`enterTxAndWait`).
2. **fw 3.6** — carrier was 68 kHz low. The module crystal measures
   **25.991 MHz**, not 26.000 (counted via PCNT on CLK_XOSC/192, no SDR).
   Trimmed FREQ to land on **433.942 MHz**, matching the FOB on the same
   dongle. Added a runtime `/rftune?khz=` trim.
3. **fw 3.7** — the stored START code came from a *long-press* capture and
   was the wrong bit pattern. Recaptured the short single-press code.
4. **fw 3.9** — no **wake-up carrier**. The Compustar receiver is duty-
   cycled; the FOB precedes its data with a ~1.44 s continuous carrier to
   wake the listener. Without it the receiver sleeps through everything —
   which is why *nothing*, lock included, ever triggered.
5. **fw 4.0** — data was sent once per carrier; the FOB sends it ~8x.

### fw 4.1 — matched the FOB's packet timing

A fresh 60 s FOB capture (one Start press + one Stop press) walked start to
finish showed the earlier "structural twin" was right on content and packet
count but **wrong on two timings**:

- The 8 data packets are spaced only **~1.1 ms** apart — back-to-back inside
  the window the carrier just woke — not the 39 ms we were using. A 35x-too-
  wide gap can let the receiver's slicer drop bit-clock lock between packets.
- The FOB holds a **~525 ms trailing carrier** *after* the 8 packets. We
  sent none.

Full FOB shape per press:
`[1.435 s carrier][~5 preamble cells][8x (3 sync + 35 bits), 1.1 ms gaps][0.525 s trailing carrier]`.

fw 4.1 makes `transmitButtonWakeup` faithful to that: `RF_START_PKT_GAP_MS = 1`,
`RF_TAIL_CARRIER_MS = 525`. We still repeat the whole unit 3x per press (the
FOB does 1) as harmless margin. Flashed via OTA; board back up on 4.1, armed.

**Still to verify on the car:** press Start and confirm crank. If 4.1 doesn't
do it, the remaining suspect is radiated power from the CC1101's little coil
antenna at range — next test is holding the board antenna within a few cm of
the windshield receiver, which separates range/antenna from protocol (and a
range problem likely vanishes once the board is installed inside the car).

## fw 4.2 / 4.3 — REMOTE START WORKS (the last two defects)

Installed the board in the car (on OBD-II 12 V via the buck) and ran a real
SDR-in-the-loop debug session. fw 4.1 still would not crank. Two more defects,
found by measuring our own board on the SDR and comparing to the FOB:

**Detour that wasted an hour (documented so it isn't repeated):** an early
capture showed a "+110 kHz carrier" I mistook for our board and "corrected"
with a −75 kHz frequency nudge — it was a stray external emitter, our board was
never there. Firing the board via `curl` at logged times (so I knew exactly
which capture window to inspect) showed the board actually transmits **strong,
on-frequency** (~+41 kHz vs the FOB's +36 kHz — 6 kHz apart, fine). Lesson:
identify our transmission by its time-structure, not by "strongest FFT bin,"
and fire it on a known schedule.

**Defect 6 (fw 4.2) — SWAPPED bit-gap timing.** Decoding our own on-air signal
proved the *bits* were bit-for-bit the FOB's Start code — yet the receiver
ignored it. Measuring per-bit timing exposed it: a Compustar bit is a pulse
**plus a matching gap** — `0` = short-HIGH + short-LOW (period ~1493 µs),
`1` = long-HIGH + long-LOW (~2243 µs). Our firmware had the two LOW values
swapped (short-HIGH + long-LOW, long-HIGH + short-LOW), making the 0 and 1
periods nearly identical (~1850 µs). A receiver decoding by period/gap can't
tell the bits apart. It hid for months because an rtl_433-style decoder reads
only the HIGH pulse, so the signal always "looked" bit-perfect. The bad numbers
came from [`sdr/analysis/framing.md`](../sdr/analysis/framing.md) (now
corrected). Fix: `CMP_SHORT_LOW_US 1136→762`, `CMP_LONG_LOW_US 756→1140`.

**Defect 7 (fw 4.3) — three bursts self-cancel.** Even with correct timing,
flat. The FOB sends exactly **one** burst per press; we sent three. A second
start command ~2.5 s later reads as a re-press and cancels the start. Set
`RF_START_BURSTS 3→1`.

**Verified end-to-end via battery telemetry** (the OBD-II tap doubles as a
start detector — engine start = a crank dip then a rise to ~14.3 V charging):
- FOB Start → 12.7 → dip 12.2 → **14.3 V** (baseline: proves receiver armed).
- Board Start (fw 4.3) → 12.7 → **dip 11.7 → rise 14.3 V**. **The board started
  the car.** A second board Start stopped it (14.3 → 13.1 V). Full start/stop.

Also fixed a stale self-test: `regs_ok` compared against pre-trim FREQ values
and always failed; updated to the fw 3.6 trim so `/rftest` reads honest.

## fw 4.4 — auto-start armed + ETA-to-auto-start

Armed the low-voltage auto-start on the installed unit (threshold 12.2 V,
hold 60 s). Added an **ETA to the next auto-start**: `autoStartEtaS()` projects
the parked battery-drain rate (same regression as the "projected to 11.8 V"
countdown) down to the trigger voltage and adds the sustain hold. Returns n/a
when disarmed, when the battery is holding/charging (won't reach the threshold),
or before the drain fit settles (~30 min parked). Surfaced three ways: a new
"Est. to auto-start" dashboard card (green when the fit is solid, amber when
`r²<0.6`), the `watching` status line ("…above 12.2 V (~2.3 days to
auto-start)"), `/json` `as_eta_s`, and SNMP OID `.44 autoStartEtaSec` for Cacti.

## fw 4.5 — graph the battery drain rate over time

Added the drain rate (mV/h) as a graphed history series, like voltage/CPU. Since
it's a computed regression (not a raw per-sample field), stored it per sample:
`Sample` gained an `int16_t drain` (PSRAM-backed ring buffer, so free), stamped

zero — the point being to *watch* the parasitic drain evolve over days. Bumped
the persisted-history magic (VOL4->VOL5) so the layout change is handled cleanly
(the saved 24 h history resets once on this upgrade, then rebuilds).

## fw 4.6 — drain tile shows honest partial progress (bugfix)

The fw 4.5 storage-format bump reset the saved history, and the drain tile then
read "0 of 30 min" and looked frozen. Cause: `computeDrain()` early-returned
`n=0` when *total* `histCount < DRAIN_MIN_N`, short-circuiting the partial-window
progress reporting it already had. Fixed the guard to `histCount < 1` so Pass 1
runs and `drain_n` reflects the real partial count (e.g. "22 of 30 min"); the
`n < DRAIN_MIN_N` guard still withholds the fitted slope until there's enough
data. Verified: `drain_n` went 0 → 22 after the flash and now climbs. (This
flash preserved history — struct unchanged, magic still VOL5.)
